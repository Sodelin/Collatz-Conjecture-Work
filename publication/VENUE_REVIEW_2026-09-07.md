## Before the next catalog-oriented research attempt

Freeze a target record before substantial proof development:

1. **Source question:** faithful statement, who posed it, primary source, version/date and exact location. Record our added restrictions separately.
2. **Chronology:** evidence that this question predates our work. Uncertain ordering stays uncertain; an old paper's publication year cannot be assigned to our newly chosen restriction.
3. **Success condition:** the exact theorem, counterexample, reduction, resolved range or bound that would answer or advance that question.
4. **Prior baseline:** what the literature already establishes and what the proposed result changes. For a bound, show the old and new values; for a qualitative implication, write the implication explicitly.
5. **Statement correspondence:** explain how the proved theorem satisfies that success condition, with all dimensions, carriers, orders, transformations, side conditions and quantifiers retained.
6. **Independent dimensions of assessment:** track proof verification, target provenance, progress, novelty evidence, venue eligibility, submission and acceptance separately.

The local [admissibility record](admissibility.json) and evaluator enforce evidence completeness and bind the assessment to the selected mathematical source and scientific statement. They do not certify historical truth, mathematical implication or originality merely because fields are filled. A retitle cannot clear a block on the same claim. Substantive changes require a fresh assessment.

## Choosing the next mathematical target

For continuity, an all-dimensional obstruction would require specifying the entire permitted matrix-interpretation class and proving a result uniformly over that class. Checking dimensions two through ten, or bounding coefficient values, does not establish that universal result. The curator's all-dimensional suggestion is a possible qualifying destination, not evidence that it is tractable.

There is an additional trap on the positive side: YAH section 4.3 establishes termination of all eleven systems obtained by deleting a single rule. A valid first full rule-removal step for the original system, followed by those known remainder results, would establish its termination and hence Collatz. Finding such a step in dimension two is therefore not a modest next milestone. This observation concerns that full rule-removal setting; it does not silently transfer to every transformed or relative-top proof architecture.

YAH Conjectures 4.11-4.14 are examples of genuinely stated targets. Conjecture 4.14 concerns a nonconvergent Collatz orbit visiting 5 or 7 modulo 8, with a related partial-map formulation in Proposition 4.15. The existing scalar certificate package does not settle that conjecture. A bounded current search did not locate a resolution; its current status would need a fresh focused check before adopting it. It is a possible feasibility study, not a promised solve.

The present report is already suitable for archival framing. A complete Lean semantics-to-constraint formalization could strengthen its verification, but would not by itself supply a previously posed question for the existing result.

## Zenodo: the initial recommendation was incomplete

Zenodo's [general policies](https://about.zenodo.org/policies/) accept many research fields, artifact types and research stages. That broad scope initially suggested a research-report deposit. It was not enough to establish eligibility: the more specific [generative-AI policy for depositors](https://support.zenodo.org/help/en-gb/13-policies/227-what-is-your-usage-policy-for-generative-ai-for-depositors), published 27 April 2026, requires underlying human-conducted research and prohibits AI tools in author, creator and contributor fields. Disclosure does not substitute for those requirements. The separate AI Usage Notice describes Zenodo's own AI use and is not the appropriate depositor rule.

The account holder explicitly states that they are not the author and do not accept an editor designation. Those roles must not be inferred from repository ownership, prompting or permission to publish. [Creator metadata are mandatory](https://help.zenodo.org/docs/deposit/describe-records/creators/) and represent a person or organization that created the resource. No truthful qualifying creator has been established for this proposed record. Calling an AI tool an organization, attributing the research to OpenAI, or changing the resource type would not solve that problem. AI-generated mathematical work should be described accurately wherever it is made public.

Zenodo's [terms](https://about.zenodo.org/terms/) also assign responsibility to the uploader; being only a depositor does not remove it. These findings concern site policy and contractual terms, not a determination that publication elsewhere is unlawful. Any renewed Zenodo attempt needs a genuinely compliant research and attribution basis or an explicit ruling from Zenodo on the fully disclosed facts. No support message has been sent.

The [portable packet](zenodo/README.md) remains useful for inspection. Proposed creator metadata are empty and explicitly unresolved. No author or editor role is assigned to the account holder in that packet. Its directory name records the attempted venue; it is not evidence of submission or acceptance. Existing rights remain intact: the repository has no blanket license, and no new broad license should be inferred from public access.

The next venue check must therefore include both scientific inclusion criteria and actual depositor requirements: AI-use policy, truthful attribution, accountability, rights and the required record fields. A general description of an archive's scope is not sufficient.

## Process-integrity assessment

**Verdict: the technical publication checks were effective; the venue-selection check failed.** The system correctly retained source identities, verification boundaries and non-claims, but allowed syntax validation and successful replay to substitute for a separate admissibility decision. The corrective control is an explicit prior-target/progress assessment before venue preparation, while preserving ordinary archive generation.

Reproducibility is assessed through source provenance, exact replay and clearly bounded claims. The later Zenodo check also exposed an incomplete policy search; the specific depositor AI rules must be checked before recommending a destination.

## Inference-robustness assessment

**Verdict: the eligibility failure is stable under more checks of the unchanged certificates.** It would change with a substantiated pre-existing target plus a proved relationship that the venue recognizes as genuine progress, or a substantially new theorem answering the broader question. A different title, a larger source archive, a fresh date or a better verification badge is insufficient.

The mathematical verdict could separately change if a semantic counterexample, invalid certificate, overlooked general theorem or exact prior result were identified. The curator's response alone establishes none of those possibilities. A Zenodo record would improve accessibility and citability; it would not resolve them.
